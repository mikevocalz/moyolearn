# The viseme field, coarticulation, and enforced closure

Machine-readable table: `viseme-classes.json`. That file is the source; this one explains it. Change the JSON, not a copy of it — `scripts/schedule-visemes.mjs` reads the JSON directly so the two cannot drift.

## Two axes, not a pose library

A per-phoneme pose lookup snaps: every phoneme gets its own keyframe, the mouth hits fifteen distinct shapes a second, and the result reads mechanical no matter how well-timed it is.

JALI's contribution (Edwards, Landreth, Fiume, Singh — ACM TOG 35(4), 2016, <https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2897824.2925984>) is to separate the two things a mouth independently does:

- **JA — jaw.** How far the mandible drops. Vowel-driven, slow, carries stress and loudness.
- **LI — lip.** What the lips do at the jaw's position: spread (`+`) through neutral to rounded (`−`). Consonant-driven, fast.

Each class in `viseme-classes.json` carries `jaw` (0..1) and `lip` (−1..+1). The two axes move at different rates on purpose: a fast consonant can change `lip` without dragging `jaw` with it, which is what lets the jaw hold through a cluster the way a real one does. A single "openness" scalar — which is what `lipFromOpenness` at `packages/avatar/src/presence/humano.ts:218` gives you today — cannot express that, and it is why energy-driven lipsync always reads as chewing.

`shape` is what the axes resolve to on named morph targets. Resolve through `shape`; do not synthesize channel values from `jaw`/`lip` at runtime.

## The channels that exist — read the manifest, not the driver

`packages/avatar/rig-manifest.json` (generated by the `avatar-rig-audit` skill) records **52 ARKit morph targets on both shipped assets** — `humano-marketing.glb` and `natalie.gltf`, identical lists, `skeletonsAgree: true`. The full ARKit set is there: `mouthPucker`, `mouthPressLeft/Right`, `mouthRollUpper/Lower`, `mouthStretchLeft/Right`, `eyeWideLeft/Right`, `tongueOut`.

The driver's `LipShape` (`packages/avatar/src/presence/humano.ts:184`) exposes **nine** of those 52. That is a limit of `lipFromOpenness`, not of the rig — it exists because the openness path has one scalar to spend and no phoneme to spend it on.

**So deliver the viseme track as `HumanoInput.face`, not as `HumanoInput.mouth`.** `face` entries are written by name through `setMorph` for every key present (`packages/avatar/src/presence/humano.ts:739`), which reaches all 52. The `mouth` scalar routes through `lipFromOpenness` and reaches nine. The wiring already exists: `tutor-avatar-3d.native.tsx:491` passes both, and `face` takes precedence when non-null.

Two channel choices in the table follow from this:

- **Rounding is `mouthPucker` plus `mouthFunnel`.** `ou` is mostly pucker; `oh` is mostly funnel with some pucker. Funnel alone gives a trumpet, not a rounded vowel.
- **Spread is `mouthStretchLeft/Right`, never `mouthSmileLeft/Right`.** Smile is AU12 and belongs to expression. Sharing the channel is exactly how a tone eats the articulation — see `face-adapter`, where the lip-corner cap lives.

Writing a channel that is not on the target still fails silently: `directEncoder` drops unknown names and `setMorph` no-ops on a missing morph. Check the manifest before adding a channel, and re-run `avatar-rig-audit` after any asset change.

## Coarticulation by overlapping dominance

Each phoneme span contributes over a window wider than itself. Weight is 1 across the phoneme's own span and falls off through raised-cosine tails outside it; overlapping windows sum, then normalize. This is the Cohen–Massaro dominance-function approach. The tail is a raised cosine because it is C¹ at its outer edge, and a discontinuous derivative shows up as a visible tick in the jaw.

**The tails are asymmetric**: the leading tail is `ANTICIPATION` (0.5) times the trailing one, in `scripts/schedule-visemes.mjs`. A symmetric window centred on the span makes every viseme lead its own phoneme by most of the half-width — measurable, and in the wrong direction for the ITU asymmetry, where a mouth that is early is far more forgiving than one that is late but is still an error. A real mouth carries a shape over into the next segment further than it anticipates the coming one, so the model matches. Anticipatory rounding before a `/u/` is the counter-example; if it reads short, raise `ou`'s `dominanceMs` rather than making the tails symmetric.

`dominanceMs` in the JSON is the tail length per class. The pattern in those numbers is deliberate:

- Vowels are widest (80–90 ms). They are the slow axis and they carry into their neighbours.
- Stops are narrowest (45–55 ms). They are transient; a wide stop smears into the vowel and softens the consonant into nothing.
- Fricatives and sibilants sit between (60–70 ms) because their acoustic duration is genuinely longer than a stop's.

**These are authored starting values, not published constants.** Calibrate them against MFA on the fixed test set and record the run — the audit is explicit that gates are "calibrated and recorded rather than claimed as established human constants."

For reference, the defaults as shipped score median 15.3 ms / p95 28.7 ms / max visual-late 28.7 ms on `references/fixtures/en-02-bilabial.phonemes.json`, self-scored — the fixture's phoneme spans stand in for MFA output. That is a check that the scheduler and the gate agree, **not** a gate result: a real result needs MFA over the whole fixed test set on a real render, and neither has been run.

### Stress

`stressGain` scales the jaw contribution by CMUdict's stress digit: primary 1.0, secondary 0.9, unstressed 0.78. Dropping stress is most of why a synthetic mouth reads flat — every syllable opens the same amount, so nothing is emphasized and the face has no rhythm. Spanish has no reduction contrast; apply gain 1.0 to every Spanish vowel and let duration carry the emphasis.

### Diphthongs

A diphthong is two spans, not one held pose. Split the phoneme's duration and schedule `class` then `target` (`AY` → `aa` then `ih`). One pose for `AY` gives a mouth that opens and stays open through "five", which is exactly the mumble that gets reported as "her mouth isn't moving".

### `HH`

`HH` has `class: null` and `inheritsNext: true`. It takes the following vowel's class at reduced dominance. It is not `sil` — scheduling it as silence closes the mouth in the middle of "how", which is worse than leaving it open.

## Bilabial closure is enforced after blending

This is the one rule that is not a blend.

`/p/ /b/ /m/` — and their Spanish counterparts — must reach a closed mouth. Dominance blending alone will not get there: a bilabial between two open vowels has both neighbours pulling the jaw open through its window, and the closure it needs lasts tens of milliseconds. The blended result is a mouth that never quite shuts, which is the single most legible lip-sync failure there is. A viewer who cannot articulate anything else about a bad mouth will notice a `/p/` that does not close.

So closure runs as a post-pass over the blended track:

1. For every span whose class carries `bilabial: true` and does not carry `noClosure`, take the window `[onset, onset + max(minClosedMs, spanDuration)]`.
2. Inside it, clamp `jawOpen ≤ closure.jawOpenCeiling` (0.06) and set `mouthClose ≥ closure.mouthCloseFloor` (0.85).
3. Zero the lip-spread and funnel channels inside the window — a closed mouth that is simultaneously smiling or funnelled is an impossible face, and `face-adapter`'s detector will flag it.
4. Ease in and out over one frame at each edge so the clamp does not introduce the derivative discontinuity the raised cosine was chosen to avoid.

`minClosedMs` is 30. A closure shorter than about two frames at 60 fps is not visible at all, so a fast `/p/` gets held slightly longer than its acoustic span. That is a deliberate visual-legibility choice, not a timing error; it lengthens the closure's *release*, never its onset, so it does not move the onset the gate measures.

### The Spanish exception

`β` (the approximant allophone of `/b/`) and `ɣ` carry `noClosure: true`. The lips approach and do not meet. Forcing closure on every orthographic `b` makes `haber`, `sabe` and `tabla` read as a stutter — which is why the pipeline reads espeak-ng's IPA rather than the spelling.

## Silence

A gap between words is `sil`, and `sil` has an empty `shape` — every channel goes to zero. Do not hold the last vowel through a pause. `analyseSpeech`'s `SILENCE = 0.02` floor exists for the same reason on the energy path (`packages/avatar/src/speech/audio-shapes.ts:37`): without a floor she chews quietly through every gap.

The driver's release ramp (`idleConfig.speech.releaseMs = 250`) handles the end of an utterance. Within an utterance, `sil` spans are scheduled like any other class.

## Output

A `Track` — `[timeSeconds, Shape][]`, time-ordered, seconds relative to the audible onset (`packages/avatar/src/speech/track.ts:28`). Sampled by the existing `sampleTrack`, which smoothsteps between keyframes. Emit at 60 keyframes per second to match `analyseSpeech`'s `FPS` and the render loop; finer buys nothing visible and costs allocation per frame.
