# The kits

Describing great design in words does not produce it. Told "layered,
lit, owned colour", a model still falls back on its habits: a big
grotesque headline, a tilted card on a block, a white receipt. The kits
are the cure: **real, rendered HTML and CSS at the level this skill must
reach**, built and checked against the work designers save to their mood
boards. Every design starts from the nearest kit's level of craft.

They live in `<skill>/assets/kits/`. Open the HTML to read the technique;
open `shots/` to see the result.

## How to use them

1. **Pick the nearest kit for each exploration** in Step 2. Three
   explorations usually start from three different kits.
2. **Read its HTML before writing yours.** The craft is in specific
   values: orbs are layered radial gradients blurred inside a clipped
   circle, not one gradient; cards glow from a radial *inside* the card;
   hairlines are 6 to 8% ink; display type is tracked tight at size.
3. **Re-skin, never copy.** New brand, new words, new data, new palette
   and type chosen for this brief (type.md, color.md). The kit's names,
   copy, numbers and images never appear in a client's work. Two clients
   must not get the same page with a different logo.
4. **Take the techniques, not the composition.** Using a kit means its
   craft (how the orb is lit, how the word meets the photo, how tight the
   type is set), not its layout. Change at least two of: where the main
   element sits, the grid, the number and order of the parts, the ratio of
   type to image. If a critic could call your page "a close descendant" of a
   kit plate, move further.
5. **Keep the level.** Before round 1, put your render next to the kit's
   shot. If yours is flatter, emptier, or more generic, it is not ready.
6. **Combine.** A landing page can use a poster hero and aura cards below;
   a dashboard can open with a serif greeting. One direction per page, but
   any kit's parts.

## Without images

Four of the five kits work with **no images at all**. When the user has no
photos and no image tool, choose from those: aura parts, the dashboard,
serif sections, the type-only poster page. Do not fill the gap with stock
cut-outs or drawings in code (finish.md §7).

## The bar for the critic

The critic is shown the kit shots (and the user's own references) as the
bar. "Would this sit next to these and hold up?" is the question behind
every score.
