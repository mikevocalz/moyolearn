## Mobbin pass: learner-today-k2

Archetype: **Scene**. Artwork ≥50% of viewport, one giant primary action slab, up to two secondary tiles.

### Purpose

The screen a 5-7 year old lands on after sign-in. It must answer four questions without reading load:

- **Where am I** — the scene itself is the answer. A recognizable place (the kitchen table), not a title bar. A child who cannot read a header still knows which app this is.
- **What matters now** — exactly one thing is big. The primary slab carries the single next activity, named in 2-4 words a 5-year-old can decode or have read aloud.
- **What can I do** — the slab plus at most two secondary tiles. Nothing else is tappable.
- **What changed after I acted** — returning from a finished activity, the scene has one visible change (an object added to the table, the manipulative left out) rather than a number that went up.

### Take

- **One stage, one cast** (Duolingo ABC room). Put the scene on a defined stage plane with the character standing on it. The stage gives the illustration a floor, which stops the art from floating and gives parallax layers something to move against.
- **Labels live inside the scene, not above it** (Kit). Activity names ride on small solid pills placed on the artwork. Keeps the art ≥50% while every target still has a readable name.
- **Hard break between scene and controls** (Finch). One decisive horizontal edge where the illustration ends and the slab begins. No fade, no gradient bleed.
- **Art first, title second, nothing third** (Spotify Kids, GoHenry). Secondary tiles get a cropped illustration plus a 2-3 word label. No progress meters, no counts.
- **Full-width bottom action carrying the plain-language description above it** (Duolingo ABC level detail). Description in a sentence a child hears once; the action repeats the verb from that sentence.
- **Character owns the frame when there is nothing due** (Tolan). Empty state is not a card that says "nothing today" — it is Natalie in the scene with one invitation.

### Open questions for critique

1. Does the "one visible change in the scene" answer to *what changed after I acted* survive a child doing three activities in a row, or does the table become cluttered? Proposal: the scene holds at most three added objects, then resets on the next day boundary.
2. Kit puts labels inside the scene; Finch puts them below a hard break. At 5-7, is the in-scene pill actually more discoverable, or does it read as scenery and go untapped? Needs a tap test, not a critique verdict.
3. If the single accent moment is a manipulative sitting in the scene, is it read as decoration rather than as the day's topic? Alternative: accent the slab's leading icon instead, and keep the scene fully neutral.
4. What does the screen look like when there is genuinely nothing assigned? Tolan's answer is the character alone. Confirm that Moyo's version offers a free-play invitation rather than an empty state.
5. Duolingo ABC's third tile is a mystery card. Is any element of surprise compatible with the no-engagement-pressure rule, or does a `?` tile smuggle in variable-reward mechanics?
6. Two secondary tiles or one? The archetype allows up to two, but every reference that stays calm at this age uses either three equal tiles or none at all.
