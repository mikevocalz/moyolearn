## 1 Scope and standard

WCAG 2.2 Level AA, plus the learner 7:1 bar wherever it is stricter. The criteria the reset actually puts at risk, and why:

| Criterion | Risk the reset introduces |
|---|---|
| 1.4.3 Contrast (minimum) | Text set over artwork. Every existing pair is text on a flat token surface; a scene has no single background value |
| 1.4.11 Non-text contrast | Interactive objects drawn *inside* a scene — a tappable paper slice has no border token |
| 2.5.8 Target size (minimum) | Same. A target drawn in art is sized by the drawing, not by a token |
| 2.3.3 Animation from interactions | The completion reveal, and the parallax the brief proposed. See §4 |
| 1.1.1 Non-text content | Art that carries the lesson is meaningful content, not decoration |
| 1.4.12 Text spacing | Hero lines set over art cannot reflow into the art's negative space if the art is a flat raster |

Not at risk and not audited here: anything the existing gates already cover on flat surfaces, which `tooling/check-contrast.mjs` and `tooling/check-targets.mjs` check on every `pnpm lint`.

## 2 Contrast, including text over artwork

### What shipped with this audit

The learner 7:1 bar was asserted in three documents and checked by nothing. `docs/site/tokens.md:271-274` records the override as out of scope for the site precisely because no learner surface renders there; every learner surface in the app is in scope, and `docs/design/art-direction.md` P5.4 restates it for text over art. `tooling/check-contrast.mjs` had no 7:1 pair at all.

Six pairs added, checked in both themes:

| Pair | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| `text` on `surface` | 19.21:1 | 16.28:1 |
| `text` on `surface-raised` | 19.54:1 | 13.94:1 |
| `text` on `surface-sunken` | 17.59:1 | 17.59:1 |
| `text-muted` on `surface` | 7.67:1 | 9.91:1 |
| `text-muted` on `surface-raised` | 7.80:1 | 8.49:1 |
| `text-muted` on `surface-sunken` | **7.02:1** | 10.72:1 |

All pass. The gate reports 187 pairs where it reported 175.

**The margin on the last row is 0.02.** `text-muted` on `surface-sunken` in light clears the learner bar by two hundredths, and until now nothing would have noticed it going under. Verified by moving `ink[600]` from `#55524A` to `#5A5750` — two ticks — which drops the pair to 6.49:1 and fails the gate by name. That is the whole reason the row is worth having.

### What still cannot be gated, and what must be written before it can

A gate can compare two token values. It cannot compare a token against a picture. Text over a scene has as many background values as it has pixels underneath it, and `docs/design/art-direction.md` states the rule (P5.4: declared pair, 4.5 body, 3 large, 7 on learner surfaces) without stating the input the rule needs.

**Blocking open question, owned by `docs/design/art-direction.md` §5.** The art direction must state one of these before any asset carrying text can enter the register:

- **A permitted luminance range for the region under text** — the darkest and lightest values an asset may place beneath a text run, as token values. The gate then checks the text token against both ends of that range, which is two ordinary pairs.
- **Or a required plate** — text over art must sit on a solid region of a named token, and the art may not extend under it. The gate then checks the text token against the plate token, which is one ordinary pair, and the rule becomes structural rather than measured.

The second is the cheaper answer and matches how the register already made missing alt impossible: put the constraint where it cannot be forgotten rather than where it has to be checked. Neither is chosen here; it belongs to the design-system and accessibility seats.

Until one is stated, `packages/art/registry.ts` has no field for it, no asset can carry text over it, and the `never` types on `scene`/`object`/`figure` mean none can land anyway. The gap is not urgent, but it must be closed before the first plate is approved rather than after.

Do not touch the `moyoHeart` / `moyoEarth` / `moyoLeaf` rows at `tooling/check-contrast.mjs:202-209`. They are declared at 3:1 with a comment stating they are large or display marks, and every use in `apps/web-vite/src` is a fill with its own paired foreground. The site doc's 4.73:1 figure for `moyoHeart` on `moyoPaper` is stale — the installed values measure 3.26:1 — but the gate is right and the table is wrong. Fix `docs/site/tokens.md`.

## 3 Target sizes

`packages/theme/tokens.ts:864-870` defines the band ladder. K–2 primary actions are `targets.young` `72px`, which is the NN/g 4× finding and is well above 2.5.8's 24px minimum and Apple's 44pt.

The new case is a target drawn inside artwork. A paper slice that is tappable is an interactive control whose hit area is defined by the drawing, and nothing in the register records it. Rules for the audit:

1. A tappable region inside a `scene` gets its hit area from the layout, never from the art's silhouette. The art may be any shape; the touch target is a rectangle from the band token.
2. Two tappable objects in one scene keep the band's minimum spacing between hit areas, even where the drawings overlap.
3. 1.4.11 applies to the object's own edge against what is behind it in the same asset. This is not gateable for the same reason as §2 and rides the same open question.

## 4 Motion

`docs/pack/02-adaptive-screens-design-spec.md:268` bans ambient and parallax motion on child surfaces outright, and `docs/decisions/adr-115-art-direction-authority.md` recommends keeping that ban whole. **Reduced-motion is therefore not the gate on a learner surface — the ban is.** `Scene`'s parallax slot goes unused on every learner band regardless of the user's motion setting.

Where motion remains:

| Motion | Where | Reduced-motion behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Press physics — slab moves 4px into its shadow | every band | No movement; the pressed state is a value change |
| Completion reveal — the object resolves | Tutor Room, K–2 completion | No animation. The resolved state renders directly, and the evidence sentence is unchanged |
| Pane reveal | adult surfaces | Instant |

2.3.3 is satisfied by each of those having a non-animated path that carries the same information. The audit checks the information, not the absence of movement: a reveal that only communicates through its animation fails even when it respects the setting.

## 5 Screen reader

### Reading order per archetype

| Archetype | Order |
|---|---|
| A Scene | Heading, then the primary slab, then the scene as one labelled image, then secondary tiles. The scene comes after the action because the action is the point and a screen-reader user should not traverse a picture to reach it |
| B Invitation | Eyebrow, H1, time cost, hero action, Up next, then the quiet list |
| C Focus | Progress, the canvas as a labelled region, the tutor's current utterance, then the action bar |
| D Day plan | Date, then rows in time order |
| E Status hero | Status sentence, evidence strip as a labelled group, the action, then details |
| F List-detail | List, then detail. The detail pane announces which row it belongs to on selection |
| G Operational table | Filter state, then the table with row and column headers |

### Alt text

`packages/art/registry.ts` carries `alt` beside each asset, so an asset with no alt is not a state the register can be put into. That is worth being precise about, because it is easy to over-claim:

**What it covers.** A missing string. A string supplied at the call site and forgotten on one of five call sites. Alt drifting from the asset when the asset is re-cropped.

**What it does not cover.** Whether the string is *right*. Whether the asset is decorative and should carry an empty alt rather than a description — the register has no decorative flag, and a scene that teaches is never decorative but an object repeated as a bullet might be. Whether the alt duplicates adjacent visible text, which is a common failure that reads as stuttering. All three are human review, and they belong on the checklist in §8 rather than in a gate.

`registry.provenance.ts` records `altSource`, the register each string is quoted from. That is the thing a reviewer checks against.

## 6 Keyboard, web adult surfaces

- Tab order follows the reading order in §5. The detail pane in archetype F is reachable without traversing the whole list.
- Focus is visible against every surface. `focus` on `surface` is gated at 3:1 (`tooling/check-contrast.mjs`), which covers the flat case and not focus over artwork — same open question as §2.
- No keyboard trap in the evidence strip; it scrolls with arrow keys and exits with Tab.
- Every action reachable by pointer is reachable by keyboard. The audit checks this per composition rather than assuming it.

## 7 What a gate catches and what a human must

| A gate catches | A human must check |
|---|---|
| A token pair under its bar, both themes | Whether text over a picture is legible at the picture's busiest point |
| A missing alt string | Whether the alt is correct, non-duplicative, and correctly empty when decorative |
| A control radius or target token off the ladder | Whether a target drawn inside art has a real hit area |
| An image referenced outside the register (`tooling/check-art-registry.mjs`) | Whether the asset earns its place at all — plate P8.1 |
| A banned list primitive, a role accent in a text slot | Reading order, focus order, and whether a reveal survives reduced motion with its meaning intact |

Being honest about the split matters more than the split itself. Four of the six criteria in §1 cannot be gated at all today, and a checklist that implies otherwise is worse than none.
