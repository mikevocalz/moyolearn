## §1 How the ratios were computed

WCAG 2.x relative luminance, `(L_lighter + 0.05) / (L_darker + 0.05)`, on the
resolved sRGB values. Alpha-composited materials were blended against the surface
they are actually drawn over before the ratio was taken — a `blendMode: 'Alpha'`
material on a `ViroFlexView` **replaces** that view's material, so it composites
over what is *behind the view*, not over the fill it visually succeeds.

## §3 Target sizes, per age band

### §3.1 The rule

```
minHitSize(d, hands, band) = 2·d·tan(4°/2) · (hands ? 1.25 : 1) · bandMultiplier[band]
```

4° is the floor the ViroReact spatial layout system sets — **not** Apple's,
Meta's or the W3C's. `bandMultiplier` is each band's ratio to `adult` in
`packages/theme/tokens.ts` `targets` (`72/44`, `56/44`, `48/44`, `1`), so the
spatial scale and the dp scale cannot drift apart. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8's 24 CSS px
is about **0.51°** by the W3C's own gloss of a CSS pixel; the house floor is
roughly eight times it, and that is the standard applied below.

### §3.2 In the spatial scene, at 1.5 m, controllers

| Band | Floor (m) | Degrees | Rail key | Ink swatch | Clear | Placement key (W × H) | Chat action (W × H) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `young` | 0.17143 | 6.54° | 0.17143 ✓ | 0.17143 ✓ | 0.17143 ✓ | 0.27429 × 0.17143 ✓ | ≤ 0.41143 × 0.17143 ✓ |
| `child` | 0.13333 | 5.09° | 0.13333 ✓ | 0.13333 ✓ | 0.13333 ✓ | 0.21333 × 0.13333 ✓ | ✓ |
| `teen` | 0.11429 | 4.36° | 0.11429 ✓ | 0.11429 ✓ | 0.11429 ✓ | 0.18286 × 0.11429 ✓ | ✓ |
| `adult` | 0.10476 | 4.00° | 0.10476 ✓ | 0.10476 ✓ | 0.10476 ✓ | 0.16762 × 0.10476 ✓ | ✓ |

**Every spatial control now sits at or above its band's floor.** That is a change
from the state this branch was committed in, where every one of them was under —
the rail clamped its key to `Math.min(width, minHitSize(…))` against a
`railWidth` of 0.1 m, and the swatches, Clear, the placement keys and the chat
actions all multiplied down from there (0.7×, 0.8×, 0.6×). The fix is in the
working tree, not in `6e5707b`.

Two notes on the shape of the fix, both of which read correctly:

- A placement key is `size × 1.6` wide and `size` tall. Wider than the floor is
  free; shorter is not, because **a target is the smaller of its two edges**. The
  earlier `size × 0.6` height cleared 4° across and missed it by 40% down.
- Clear is a full-size key behind a `spatialSpacing.md` gap rather than a
  three-quarter-size key behind an `sm` one. Harder to hit by accident is
  *separation*; a smaller Clear is also harder to hit on purpose, and a child who
  misses it twice hits it on the third try anyway.

### §3.3 The 2D entry pill — under its band on two of four

`XrBoardButton` declares no `min-h-target-*`. Its own header says "the target
comes from the age band, never from a number here … (`tooling/check-targets.mjs`)",
and that gate scopes `COMPONENTS` to `packages/ui/Button.tsx` alone, so nothing
has ever checked the claim.

The height is therefore whatever padding, glyph and line box add up to. The
component uses the **unsuffixed** spacing and type utilities, and
`packages/theme/build-css.mjs:120-125` emits the unsuffixed variable as the
**cool** value — so a learner surface gets the ops density. At `rem: 16`
(`apps/mobile/metro.config.js:150`):

```
border-2            = 2 + 2                       = 4
py-element (cool)   = 0.5rem × 2 = 8 × 2          = 16
py-group   (cool)   = 1.5rem × 2 = 24 × 2         = 48
text-caption (cool) = 0.75rem × 1.35 = 12 × 1.35  = 16.2   (the line box)
```

| `size` | Band | Height | Band floor | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `md` | `adult` | `4 + 16 + max(18 glyph, 16.2) = 38` | 44 | **under by 6dp** |
| `lg` | `teen` | `4 + 16 + max(20, 16.2) = 40` | 48 | **under by 8dp** |
| `xl` | `child` | `4 + 48 + max(24, 16.2) = 76` | 56 | pass |
| `xl` | `young` | `4 + 48 + 24 = 76` | 72 | pass |

All four clear WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8's 24 CSS px, so this is **not** an AA failure.
It is a failure of the product's own floor, and that floor exists because 24px is
not a target for a child's hand. The `sm` size is declared and never passed by
any caller today.

The arithmetic assumes the `Text` line box is the content height and that
`PressScale` adds no `minHeight` or `hitSlop` — both confirmed by reading
`press-scale.native.tsx`. It has not been measured on a device.

## §4 Findings, with severity and fix

### F1 · The hover ring is invisible — **blocker**

`XR_COLOR.focus` and `chrome.key` are both `palette.ink[100]`, so a key's focus
ring is `#F6F3E8` drawn on `#F6F3E8`: **1.00:1**. Affects every `RailKey`
(`XrRail.native.tsx:92-93`) and every `ControlKey`
(`XrOrnaments.native.tsx:78-79`).

SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast (AA) and SC 2.4.7 Focus Visible (AA), by analogy —
this ring *is* the focus indicator, and in a headset a hover highlight is often
the only feedback a ray gives before a press lands. There is no cursor to fall
back on.

**Fix.** Point `XR_COLOR.focus` at a token that contrasts with the key rather
than matching it. `02-design-plan.md` §6.2 already names the selection/focus
collision and proposes `semantic.focus`, which needs one correction: `focus` in
this repo is `gold[500]` light / `gold[400]` dark, and the scene uses the dark
values throughout (`xr-colors.ts:17-18`). Measured against the key:

| Candidate | Value | vs key `ink[100]` | vs rail `ink[800]` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `semantic.focus.dark` (`gold[400]`) | `#5C8AFF` | **2.89:1** — under 3:1 | 4.82:1 |
| `semantic.focus.light` (`gold[500]`) | `#3B6DF6` | **4.02:1** | 2.09:1 |
| `semantic['border-strong'].light` | `#000000` | **18.90:1** | 1.26:1 |
| `semantic['surface-sunken'].dark` (`ink[950]`) | `#0D0C0B` | **17.59:1** | 1.14:1 |

The ring is drawn *on the key*, so the key is the adjacent colour that matters
and `focus.light` is the one `focus` value that clears 3:1 there. Using the light
step for one prop in an otherwise-dark scene needs a written reason, or the ring
becomes a near-black outline (`ink[950]`, 17.59:1) and `focus` is left for the
2D product. Either choice beats 1.00:1; the one to avoid is `focus.dark`, which
looks like the obvious fix and is 2.89:1.

### F2 · Selection is not perceivable on a tool key — **blocker**

`XR_MATERIAL.keySelected` is registered with `chrome.focus`, and
`XR_MATERIAL.key` with `chrome.key`. Both are `ink[100]`. So `selected` swaps a
key's fill from `#F6F3E8` to `#F6F3E8`.

The rail's stated rule is four redundant signals — fill, inverted label colour,
a ring, and stable position. Today: the fill does not change, the label colour
does not change (both states use `XR_COLOR.onKey`), and the ring is F1. **The
only surviving signal is position**, and position cannot tell a child *which* of
Pen, Mark and Erase is active, because all three are always in the same place.

SC 1.4.1 Use of Colour (A) in the strict sense is not violated — no colour
carries it either — but SC 1.4.11 is, and the practical outcome is worse than a
colour-only cue: there is no cue.

**Fix.** Register `keySelected` against a distinct token. `semantic.highlighter`
is the product's "you are here" marker and is what `02-design-plan.md` §6.2
proposes; its dark step is `lagoon[500]` `#12A7A3`, which measures **5.22:1**
against the rail and carries `on-highlighter` (`ink[950]`) at **6.59:1**. Against
the resting key it is 2.67:1 — a hue change rather than a luminance one, which is
enough to read as a different state on a slab this size but is worth confirming
on a device (§8).

Whatever fill is chosen, keep the label inversion honest: if the selected fill
goes dark the label has to go light, which is the rule `XrRail.native.tsx:112-114`
already documents and which is currently a no-op because both states are the same
colour.

### F3 · Choosing an ink colour is a colour-only decision — **blocker**

`XrRail.native.tsx:212` renders each swatch with `label=""`. Seven keys whose
only difference is their fill. A colour-blind child cannot tell red from green,
and a child using a greyscale accessibility filter cannot tell any of them apart.

SC 1.4.1 Use of Colour, Level A. This is the one failure in the feature that
the rail's own header says the four-signal rule exists to prevent.

**Fix.** Give each swatch the colour's name, from `Whiteboard.tsx`'s `INKS` — the
seven ids are already `'black' … 'violet'`. Position is stable, so the name plus
the fill is two signals. `04-copy.md` §4 carries this as a copy flag with a
proposed fix; it is a WCAG failure as well as a copy one.

### F4 · A pressed key's label drops to 2.21:1 — **should-fix**

`moyoKeyPressed` is `#FFFDF7` at 20% alpha with `blendMode: 'Alpha'`. Setting it
as a `ViroFlexView`'s material replaces the resting material, so it composites
over the **rail** (`#262420`), not over the key it replaces — giving `#514F4B`.
The label stays `XR_COLOR.onKey` (`#171614`), which is **2.21:1** on that fill.

The material was designed for a dark key ("a 20% white wash, the spatial
equivalent of the 2D pressed state"). The key became light when `chrome.key` was
pointed at `ink[100]`, and the pressed material did not follow.

Severity is should-fix rather than blocker because the state is transient and the
fill change itself is perceptible (7.36:1 against the resting key) — but a press
that makes the label unreadable is a press a child cannot confirm landed on the
key they meant.

**Fix.** Make `keyPressed` a darkening of the light key rather than a lightening
of a dark one — `ink[950]` at low alpha, or an opaque `ink[200]` — and keep the
label at `XR_COLOR.onKey`.

On `XrPlacementControls` the same material composites over **nothing**: the row's
container is `materials={[]}`, so a pressed Recenter or Leave blends against
whatever the passthrough camera is showing. That is unmeasurable by computation
and belongs in §8.

### F5 · A disabled key has no visible body — **should-fix**

`moyoKeyDisabled` is `#0D0C0B` at 50% over the rail → `#1A1816`, **1.14:1**
against the rail it sits on. The label on it is `ink[300]` at 9.71:1 and reads
fine, so what a child sees is the word "Undo" floating on the rail with no key
under it.

SC 1.4.11. Disabled controls are exempt from the contrast minimum in WCAG's own
note, so this is not strictly a failure — but "there is no key here" and "the key
here is off" are different messages, and the second is the true one.

**Fix.** A disabled key should read as the same object, dimmed — an opaque mid
step of the same ramp rather than a near-transparent near-black. `ink[400]`
`#94917F` measures **4.88:1** against the rail (the key still has a body) and
2.86:1 against the resting key (it reads as the same object, dulled), and carries
`XR_COLOR.onKey` at 5.70:1 so the label does not have to change colour at all.

### F6 · The wait spinner is `type="dark"` on near-black — **should-fix**

During `checking` and `preparing` the surface is not drawn, so the spinner and
the wait line sit in front of the frame quad, which is `chrome.frame` =
`ink[950]` `#0D0C0B`. The text was already corrected to `XR_COLOR.onPanel`
(19.21:1). `XrPanel.native.tsx:391` still asks for `ViroSpinner type="dark"`.

SC 1.4.11 for a status indicator. The exact contrast cannot be computed —
`ViroSpinner`'s two types are built-in textures whose pixel values are not
exposed in the installed package's types — but "dark" on `#0D0C0B` is the wrong
end of the pair by name.

**Fix.** `type="light"`, matching the text beside it. Confirm the rendered
texture on a device.

### F7 · The pill's accessible name does not contain its visible label — **should-fix**

```tsx
aria-label="Open spatial whiteboard"
…
<Text variant="caption">{entering ? 'Opening…' : 'XR'}</Text>
```

(`XrBoardButton.tsx:71, 76`.) SC 2.5.3 Label in Name, Level A: the accessible
name must contain the text that is presented visually. "XR" does not appear in
"Open spatial whiteboard", so a voice-control user who says "tap XR" gets
nothing, and a screen-reader user hearing "Open spatial whiteboard" cannot match
it to the "XR" they can see.

The comment at `:67-70` argues for the name — "what it does, not what it is … a
child looking for their board is looking for their board" — and the argument is
right about the *visible* label rather than about the accessible one. The fix is
to move it: make the visible word the one a child would say.

**Fix.** Change the visible label to match the name rather than the other way
round — "Board in space", or "Space" for the narrow rail — and keep
`aria-label="Open spatial whiteboard"`. That satisfies 2.5.3, and it also
answers `04-copy.md`'s objection that "XR" is a technology name a seven-year-old
has no reason to know.

### F8 · The entry pill declares no target floor — **should-fix**

§3.3. The `adult` and `teen` bands render 6 and 8 dp under their band target, the
component carries no `min-h-target-*`, and `tooling/check-targets.mjs` does not
look at it.

**Fix.** Two parts, and both are needed:

1. Take the class from the band, not from a size prop — `BAND_SCALE[band].target`
   is the value, exactly as every other learner control does it.
2. Widen `check-targets.mjs`'s `COMPONENTS` list so the claim in the component's
   own header is enforced. A gate that checks one file while four components
   make the same promise is a gate that certifies the file it happens to name.

### F9 · The rail overflows its own box — **blocker, and not primarily an a11y one**

At the `young` band the rail's content is 1.4714 m closed and 2.6714 m open
inside a 0.72 m content box (`05-handoff.md` §2.6). Yoga's default `flexShrink`
is 0, so the expected behaviour is overflow rather than compression.

It appears here because of what it does to reach: if Clear and the open ink
column are drawn outside the rail slab, they may be unhittable, partly occluded,
or drawn over the paper — and the argument that makes Clear safe without a
confirmation dialog ("Undo is on the rail directly above") stops holding the
moment Undo is off screen.

**Fix.** Belongs to layout, not to a11y: fewer keys, a second column, or a
scrolling rail. Whichever is chosen, the acceptance condition is that Undo and
Clear are both inside the slab at the `young` band with the ink picker open.

## §5 Screen readers, and what this medium does not have

### §5.1 In the scene: nothing

`ViroText` and `ViroFlexView` expose no `accessibilityLabel`, no `accessibilityRole`
and no `accessible` prop in the installed package. **Nothing in the spatial scene
is announced, and no assistive technology can reach it.** Every control is a quad
that answers a ray.

That is a platform limit rather than a choice, and it has three consequences that
should be stated rather than discovered:

- SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value cannot be satisfied inside the scene by anything
  this codebase can write. The scene is not conformant and cannot be made
  conformant against the installed renderer.
- Therefore the 2D board is not an "alternate version" for a blind child — it is
  **the** version. The spatial board has to stay an alternative presentation that
  a learner loses nothing by ignoring, which is exactly what `XrBoardButton`'s
  header already argues for the pill's prominence. That argument is now a
  conformance requirement as well as a design one.
- SC 2.4.2 Page Titled and SC 2.4.3 Focus Order have no meaning in the scene.
  What replaces focus order is reach order, and it is documented in
  `05-handoff.md` §8.

### §5.2 On the entry pill: adequate, with F7 outstanding

`PressScale` forwards `role="button"`, `aria-label` and `aria-disabled`, and the
pill passes all three through (`XrBoardButton.tsx:66-72`). `aria-disabled` is set
while `entering` and `onPress` is simultaneously set to `undefined`, so the
control is announced as disabled *and* actually inert — the two agree, which is
the common failure here and is avoided.

The state change is not announced. A child who presses the pill sees it change to
"Opening…" and hears nothing; SC 4.1.3 Status Messages (AA) would want a live
region. Severity: low, because the route push follows within the same interaction
and the destination announces itself.

**`available={false}` renders nothing at all** (`:60`). That is correct and worth
defending: an offer a child cannot take, announced to a screen reader as a
disabled button with no explanation, is worse than no offer. Nothing there is
fixable by the child.

## §6 Controller, hand and gaze parity

| Input | Reaches the scene | Target rule applied | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Controller ray | yes — every `onClickState` / `onDrag` path | `minHitSize(d, false, band)` | none |
| Hand (pinch) | yes, through the same events | `handMultiplier` 1.25 exists and **is never applied** | `handsPrimary` is hardcoded `false` in `tutor-xr-screen.native.tsx` |
| Eye gaze | `XrSurfaceInput.source` carries the input-source id and nothing reads it | none | untested; no dwell, no confirm |

**Hands are the real gap, and it is deliberate.** The comment at the hardcode is
correct that the smaller multiplier is the one that must not be assumed, so
assuming controllers is the conservative direction *for sizing*. But the
consequence is that a child who only has hands gets controller-sized targets:
0.17143 m where the rule says 0.21429 m at the `young` band, a 25% shortfall on
every control in the scene.

**Fix.** Read the runtime's input source and pass it. Until then, this is a known
degradation for hand-tracking users and should be recorded as one rather than
left as a constant with a comment. `spatial-tokens.ts` has already made the right
call about what happens next: at the `young` band on hands, `railWidthFor` is
0.26429 m, which the composition cannot hold, and `layoutBoard` reports
`miss: 'rail-below-target'` rather than shrinking a six-year-old's controls. That
path is correct and has never run.

**Gaze** is the one to be careful about. Nothing in the feature implements dwell
or a gaze-confirm, and `source` is carried through the contract and discarded. A
gaze-primary child cannot use this surface at all today. That is a scope
statement, not a defect — but it should be written down, because
`XrSurfaceInput.source`'s doc comment ("a controller ray, a hand, or eye gaze")
currently implies three supported inputs where there is one.

## §7 Motion

Nothing in `packages/ui/xr` animates. No transition, no idle float, no pulse, no
parallax. **There is therefore no current violation of SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide
or SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions**, and nothing to switch off.

`XrPanelProps.reducedMotion` is declared and never destructured
(`XrPanel.native.tsx:102-114`). That is not a defect today; it is the carrier for
the one animation `02-design-plan.md` §5 specifies — the board arriving, 0.94 →
1.0 over 300 ms on the `preparing → ready` edge. When that lands:

- Reduced Motion resolves it to its final frame. The board is simply present.
  `useReducedMotion()` already resolves Natalie to `audio-only` on the 2D screen,
  so the signal exists and only has to reach here.
- The camera is never touched and the anchor never travels across the room. The
  comfort rule is already written into `XrOrnaments.native.tsx`: the only thing
  that moves the view is the person wearing the headset. A vestibular-sensitive
  child is harmed by camera motion far more than by a panel fade, and this
  feature has none.
- Recenter should travel over 200 ms rather than teleport, and that travel is
  also motion a reduced-motion setting should collapse.

## §8 What needs a device, and cannot be answered here

1. **Perceived contrast through passthrough in a lit room.** Every ratio in §2 is
   a token computation. A passthrough display's black level, its exposure
   response and the room's own light all sit between these numbers and a child's
   eye, and none of the three is in this repo.
2. **Whether `#FFFFFF` paper at 21° × 29° is a glare source** at 1.5 m. An opaque
   near-white rectangle is the one element large enough to change the room's
   apparent brightness.
3. **Whether a 0.004 m hover ring is visible at all.** It is 0.15° wide. Fixing
   F1's colour does not make it a legible width, and it may need to be thicker,
   an inset, or a fill change instead of a ring.
4. **What `ViroSpinner type="light"` actually draws.** F6's fix has to be seen.
5. **What a pressed `ControlKey` composites against.** F4's second half: the
   placement row has no material behind it, so the answer is the passthrough
   camera and it is different in every room.
6. **Whether `spatialFontSize.body` = 5 pt renders at 0.05 m.** If the renderer's
   `kTextPointToWorldScale` does not hold, every string in the feature is wrong
   by the same factor and no contrast ratio here matters, because nothing is
   legible at any ratio.
7. **Whether a 4° floor is the right floor.** It is the ViroReact layout system's
   number, not Apple's, Meta's or the W3C's, and the whole target table rests on
   it.

## §9 Priority

1. **F1 + F2 together** — one token change fixes both, and between them they are
   why a child cannot tell which tool is selected or where their ray is pointing.
2. **F3** — the ink swatches. A Level A failure, in the one place the codebase
   wrote down the rule that prevents it.
3. **F9** — the rail overflow, because it decides whether Undo and Clear exist on

4. F8, then F7, then F4, F5, F6.
